From a Single Demonstration to a General Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Xing Li Oliver Brock

Robotics and Biology Laboratory / Science of Intelligence

The International Journal of Robotics Research · 2026

Paper Code BibTeX

We build a policy for multi-stage contact-rich manipulation from a single human demonstration. Environmental constraints are the inductive bias: they separate a general policy template, shared by all task instances, from instance-specific details such as poses and geometry, which compliant execution completes at run time. With self-guided augmentation and a few corrections, the policy generalizes to unseen poses and geometries on seven real-world tasks, with over 90% success.

How it works

General policy template + instance-specific completion

A policy generalizes when learning captures only what all task instances share, as a general policy template, and leaves what differs to be completed during execution.

Environmental constraints are the inductive bias

Objects built for the same function share the same constraints: drawers slide, handles turn, keys insert and then rotate. Their geometry differs, but the sequence of constraints and contact transitions is the same, so that is what we learn.

The instance-specific details are completed at run time: a compliant controller lets the constraints guide the motion, and the resulting forces and motions reveal the geometry.
